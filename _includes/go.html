How it works

Read, sit the exam, get a certificate

  1. Create an account. Name, email, password. The name is what goes on the certificate.
  2. Read the lessons. Written, self-paced, about an hour. Progress saves as you go.
  3. Take the exam. It unlocks when every lesson is done: {{ f0.exam.questions }} multiple-choice questions, timed, scored the moment you submit. You see per-module results, not the answer key.
  4. Share the certificate. A Wirewalk certificate of completion with a unique ID; anyone can check it at /verify, and you can add it to LinkedIn.

Fair questions

Is it really free?

Yes. The intro courses cost $0, including the exam and the certificate. You create an account (name, email, password) and we do not ask for a card. {{ page.free_extra }}

What's the catch: we hope some people take a paid course afterwards. If you don't, that's fine; the free course is complete on its own.

Who made this?

The Wirewalk Institute of AI, the training arm of Wirewalk, built by the team behind Wirewalk's AI and HPC infrastructure practice. It is independent: not a reseller, partner or official training provider for OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, NVIDIA or any other vendor, and no vendor sponsors or reviews the courses. About the Institute.

Is the certificate official?

Be clear about what it is: a Wirewalk Institute of AI certificate of completion and assessment. It shows you finished the course and passed its {{ f0.exam.questions }}-question, automatically scored exam. It is not a vendor certification, not accredited, and not academic credit, and we make no claim that employers recognise it. Its ID lets anyone confirm we issued it, at institute.wirewalk.com/verify. What it does and doesn't show.

What if I fail the exam?

You see your score per module so you know what to re-read, and you can try again after 24 hours, up to {{ f0.exam.attempts }} attempts. Questions and options are reshuffled on every attempt.

What this site keeps. If you came from an ad, the campaign tags in the link (utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content, utm_term and, if present, Reddit's click ID) are saved in your browser for up to 30 days and sent with your sign-up if you create an account, so we can see which ads lead to sign-ups; the browser copy is deleted once you sign up. Page views and button clicks here are counted as anonymous daily totals: no cookies, no IP address or user ID in those counts. There is no Reddit pixel or other ad tracker on this site. An account stores your name, email and learning records. Privacy policy.
