Certification

An exam you have to earn, and a certificate that says exactly what it means

Every course ends in a timed, automatically scored exam. Pass it and you receive a certificate of completion and assessment that anyone can check against our public register.

Assessment

How the exam works

  1. Earned access

    The exam unlocks only after you have completed every lesson in the course.

  2. A fresh paper

    Questions are drawn from a larger bank, spread across every module, and the answer options are shuffled for each attempt.

  3. Timed and scored

    The clock runs on our server. Answers are scored automatically on submission, and the pass mark is shown on each course page — 80% for every current course.

  4. Retakes with a pause

    Miss the mark and you can try again after 24 hours, up to three attempts per course.

The rules, in full

  • Question count and time limit are set per course and listed on its page: typically 20 questions in 40–45 minutes at Basic and Intermediate, 25 in 50–60 at Expert, and 15 in 30 at Executive.
  • Questions with several correct answers score only when you select exactly the right set — no part marks.
  • The deadline is firm. A short grace period covers network delay; a paper submitted later than that is scored as unanswered.
  • Interrupted? Opening the exam again resumes your open attempt with the same clock rather than starting a new one.
  • Feedback after each attempt shows your score per module, so you know what to revise. We never release the answer key.

Why no answer key?

The bank is reused across attempts and learners. Publishing answers would make every later certificate worth less — including yours. Module-level feedback tells you where to look; the lessons have the rest.

The certificate

What it attests — and what it does not

It attests that

  • The account holder completed every lesson of the named course.
  • They passed its timed exam, with the score and date shown.
  • The certificate was issued by the Wirewalk Institute of AI and has not been revoked, as the verification page confirms.

It does not attest that

  • The holder's identity was checked. The name is the one on the learner's account when they passed.
  • The exam was proctored. It is taken online, unsupervised.
  • The holder is competent in a workplace. A multiple-choice exam samples knowledge; it does not observe practice.
  • Any vendor recognizes it. It is not a certification from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, NVIDIA or anyone else.
  • It is accredited. It is not an academic credit, degree, license or regulated qualification.

We may revoke a certificate issued in error or obtained by misconduct. A revoked certificate shows as revoked on its verification page.

Open Badges

Your certificate as an Open Badge

Open Badges 3.0 is a published, open standard (from the standards body 1EdTech) for digital credentials that software can check. When you are signed in, your certificate page lets you download your certificate as a badge file: a .json file, or an .svg badge image with the same data inside. You can import either into a wallet or backpack that supports Open Badges 3.0. The file is digitally signed by the Wirewalk Institute of AI, so anyone who checks it can confirm that we issued it, that it has not been altered, and whether it has since been revoked.

A badge is a file format, not a seal of approval. It says exactly what the certificate says and keeps every limit listed above: it is not an accreditation, academic credit, continuing education units (CEUs) or a license, and issuing it in this format does not mean that 1EdTech, a vendor or anyone else endorses the course.

The file contains the name on the certificate, the course or program and what it took to earn it, the issue, renewal and expiry dates, the exam score, a position in our public revocation list, and your email address as a salted hash. The hash does not show the address, but the salt is in the file too, so anyone who knows or guesses the address can check it against the badge. When you check a badge's email hash, enter the address in lowercase: we hash the lowercase form. The file never contains the address itself or any other account details. We issue badges only for accounts whose email address has been verified.

The file also contains a refresh link. Anyone who has the file can use it to download the current version of the badge, for as long as the certificate is valid. If the name on a certificate is corrected, the link in older copies stops working, and the holder downloads the badge again. The newest copy has the latest "valid from" date. The verification page stays the live record: if a certificate is revoked, the badge stops checking as valid too.

Sample

What you receive

A landscape certificate you can print or save as a PDF, showing your name, the course, the issue date, your exam score and a unique certificate ID. It links to its own verification page, and its page in your learner account has a one-click “Add to LinkedIn” link.

Complete every course in a certification program and a program certificate is issued automatically, with its own ID.

The name, ID, course and score shown are placeholders.

Verification

Anyone can check a certificate in seconds

Enter the certificate ID, or follow the link printed on it. The page shows the holder's name, the course or program, the issue date, the score, and whether the certificate is valid, revoked or expired. Some certificates carry a "valid until" date; an expired course certificate is renewed, under the same ID, by passing the course exam again. It shows nothing else — no email address, no account details.
